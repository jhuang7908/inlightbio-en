Nature · Oct 28, 2026

First-in-class METTL1 inhibitor STM9005 cuts tRNA m7G and slows AML and solid-tumor models

Starting from a ~250,000-compound high-throughput screen, the team optimized the hit STM4529 into STM9005, a METTL1–WDR4 inhibitor with >10,000-fold selectivity. STM9005 lowers m7G on a subset of tRNAs, reduces translation of oncogenic mRNAs such as CDK4, blocks growth of leukaemia and solid-tumor cell models, and prolongs survival in AML mouse and PDX models.

By Jing Huang, PhD · Generated: Nov 1, 2026

Citation

Yankova E, Sapetschnig A, Thomas B, Evans S, Eleftheriou M, Gozdecka M, et al. Pharmacological inhibition of METTL1 as a therapy against solid and blood cancers. Nature. 2026 Oct 28. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11178-z

Primary field: Nucleic acid & gene therapy · Related: RNA modification, tRNA m7G, METTL1–WDR4, Acute myeloid leukaemia, Small-molecule discovery

About the authors

46 authors; see the paper for affiliations.

Corresponding authors: Oliver Rausch, Konstantinos Tzelepis

Key data card

  • Study type: Preclinical drug discovery (biochemistry, cell models, mouse and PDX models)
  • Starting point: ~250,000-compound HTS; hit STM4529
  • Compound: STM9005, METTL1–WDR4 inhibitor
  • Selectivity: >10,000-fold vs a broad panel of methyltransferases
  • Effect: Lower m7G on a subset of tRNAs; reduced translation of oncogenic mRNAs (e.g. CDK4)
  • Models: Leukaemia, melanoma, pancreatic, oesophageal and small cell lung carcinoma cell models; AML mouse and PDX models
  • Evidence level: Preclinical; no human data
  • Mechanism: inhibiting METTL1 depletes m7G-modified tRNAs, which selectively lowers translation of growth-related mRNAs.

Background and open questions

METTL1, with its partner WDR4, installs N7-methylguanosine (m7G) on tRNAs. It is overexpressed in several haematological and solid malignancies, but no selective pharmacological inhibitor had been described, so it was unclear whether targeting the enzyme is tractable and tolerated.

Study design

A high-throughput screen of about 250,000 compounds produced the starting hit STM4529, which medicinal chemistry optimized into STM9005. The team profiled selectivity, tRNA modification, translation and growth in cell models, then tested efficacy in AML mouse and patient-derived xenograft (PDX) models.

Key results

  • STM9005 showed greater than 10,000-fold selectivity for METTL1–WDR4 in a broad methyltransferase panel.
  • Treatment reduced m7G on a subset of tRNAs and lowered translation of oncogenic transcripts including CDK4.
  • STM9005 blocked growth of leukaemia, melanoma, pancreatic carcinoma, oesophageal cancer and small cell lung carcinoma cell models.
  • In AML mouse and PDX models, treatment prolonged survival.

Mechanistic interpretation

The data support a model in which cancers that depend on m7G-modified tRNAs for efficient decoding of specific mRNAs are vulnerable when the modification is lost.

Limitations

  • All data are preclinical; human safety, dosing and efficacy are unknown.
  • Several authors are affiliated with or hold interests in Storm Therapeutics, which develops METTL1 inhibitors.
