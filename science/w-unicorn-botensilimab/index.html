UNICORN trial: Fc-enhanced anti-CTLA-4 botensilimab in neoadjuvant colon cancer — dMMR combination MPR 100% (14/14)

Short-course preoperative Fc-enhanced anti-CTLA-4 botensilimab, alone or combined with anti-PD-1 balstilimab, was evaluated in resectable locally advanced pMMR or dMMR colon cancer. Major pathological response (MPR) rates were 0% (0/14) and 36% (5/14) in the pMMR monotherapy and combination cohorts and 36% (5/14) and 100% (14/14) in the corresponding dMMR cohorts; pCR was 93% (13/14) in the dMMR combination cohort. ctDNA clearance correlated with pathological response.

Background and open questions

Checkpoint inhibitors have transformed treatment of microsatellite-unstable (dMMR) colorectal cancer, but microsatellite-stable (pMMR) tumors—which represent 85% of cases—remain largely resistant. Fc-enhanced anti-CTLA-4 antibodies like botensilimab are designed to more potently deplete regulatory T cells and enhance antitumor immunity. Whether this mechanism could unlock responses in pMMR colon cancer was a key question.

Study design

The UNICORN trial was a nonrandomized phase 2 window-of-opportunity study enrolling 56 patients with resectable locally advanced colon cancer across four sequentially allocated cohorts of 14 patients each: pMMR receiving botensilimab alone, pMMR receiving the combination with balstilimab, dMMR receiving botensilimab alone, and dMMR receiving the combination. The primary endpoint was major pathological response (MPR).

Key results

  • In pMMR patients, MPR was 0% (0/14) with botensilimab monotherapy and 36% (5/14) with the combination; pCR was 0% and 29% (4/14), respectively.
  • In dMMR patients, MPR was 36% (5/14) with monotherapy and 100% (14/14) with the combination; pCR was 29% (4/14) and 93% (13/14), respectively.
  • All but the pMMR monotherapy cohort met the primary endpoint.
  • Immune-related adverse events occurred in 8/28 (29%) with botensilimab alone and 15/28 (54%) with the combination, mostly flu-like symptoms and diarrhea; no adverse-event-related deaths.
  • ctDNA clearance correlated with pathological response.

Mechanistic interpretation

The Fc-enhanced design of botensilimab may provide stronger Treg depletion and immune activation than conventional anti-CTLA-4 antibodies. The combination with PD-1 blockade appears necessary to generate responses in the historically resistant pMMR population, though the effect is still modest compared with dMMR tumors.

Limitations

  • The study uses small sequential cohorts and is not a randomized treatment comparison.
  • The pMMR combination response (36% MPR) is encouraging but remains lower than the dMMR combination (100%).
  • Long-term outcomes such as recurrence-free and overall survival are not yet available.
