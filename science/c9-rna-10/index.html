Shotgun genetic engineering rebuilds essential amino acid synthesis in mammalian cells

Shotgun genetic engineering (SGE) delivers large numbers of barcoded small constructs into mammalian cells at high multiplicity, so that each cell carries its own pathway variant; screening millions of combinations produced CHO cells that double in about 1.1 days without valine, and Jurkat cells that also grow without valine.

Citation

Trolle J, Sessa S, Wudzinska A, Grivainis M, Rincones K, Rodrick T, et al. Highly multiplexed mammalian metabolic engineering with a shotgun approach. Nat Biotechnol. 2026 Oct 6. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-026-03318-7

Primary field: Nucleic acid & gene therapy · Related: Metabolic engineering, Synthetic biology, CHO cells, Barcoded libraries, Mitochondrial targeting, Machine learning

About the authors

First author Julie Trolle, with senior authors Sudarshan Pinglay and Jef D. Boeke.

Key data card

  • Study type: In vitro screening and metabolic engineering in mammalian cell lines (Nature Biotechnology, open access)
  • Sample size n: Millions of pathway combinations screened (as reported in the abstract)
  • Controls: Cells engineered by conventional rational pathway design, and growth in amino-acid-replete medium
  • Intervention/dose: High-multiplicity delivery of barcoded small transcription units, followed by selection in valine-free or isoleucine-free medium
  • Primary endpoint: Growth in medium lacking the target essential amino acid, and recovery of the transcription-unit barcodes from surviving cells
  • Primary endpoint result: Near-wild-type growth without valine in CHO cells, with a doubling time of about 1.1 days; isoleucine-free growth in CHO cells; valine-free growth in Jurkat cells
  • Statistics: Barcode sequencing of phenotypically selected populations; the resulting datasets were used to train a machine-learning classifier of functional pathways
  • Evidence level: Full text
  • High-throughput delivery of barcoded small constructs
  • Functional pathways recovered by phenotype
  • Near-wild-type valine-free growth in CHO cells
  • Functional pathways favour mitochondrial localization
Quick look

Barcoded single transcription units were delivered at high multiplicity so that each cell assembled a different synthetic pathway, and functional pathways were recovered by sequencing barcodes from cells that survived amino acid dropout. Millions of pathway combinations were screened in Chinese hamster ovary cells to reconstitute valine and isoleucine biosynthesis; the best valine clones doubled in about 1.1 days in valine-free medium. Functional pathways favoured mitochondrial localization of the enzymes and required tens of kilobases of integrated synthetic DNA. The same strategy produced valine-free growth in Jurkat cells.

Background and open questions

Mammalian metabolic engineering is limited by the size of the combinatorial design space and by the difficulty of building and delivering very long DNA constructs. As a pathway grows, the number of possible choices of coding sequence, promoter strength, stoichiometry and subcellular location rises far faster than the number of constructs that can be made and tested one at a time.

SGE reframes the problem: rather than one large construct per experiment, many barcoded small transcription units are delivered to each cell, so every cell becomes an independent experiment carrying its own combination of pathway parts. Cells with the desired phenotype are then sequenced to recover which combination worked.

Study design

Barcoded transcription units were delivered at high multiplicity into mammalian cells, varying gene content, stoichiometry and organellar localization. Essential amino acid biosynthesis provided a stringent and directly selectable test: cells were grown without the target amino acid, so only those carrying a functional pathway survived. Barcodes were then sequenced from the surviving cells to identify the pathway combinations responsible.

Key results

Valine prototrophy at close to wild-type growth

Screening millions of pathway combinations in Chinese hamster ovary cells reconstituted and then optimized valine biosynthesis, giving clones with a doubling time of about 1.1 days in valine-free medium, close to growth in valine-replete medium and well ahead of pathways built by rational design.

Isoleucine prototrophy, previously out of reach

The same screen also produced a functional isoleucine biosynthesis solution in CHO cells, a phenotype that rational design had not achieved despite the overlap in enzymatic steps with the valine pathway.

Localization and construct scale

Functional pathways consistently favoured mitochondrial localization of the biosynthetic enzymes, and required the integration of tens of kilobases of synthetic DNA, beyond the scale practical for conventional construct-by-construct screening.

Transfer to a human T cell line

Valine prototrophy was also established in Jurkat cells, an initial demonstration that the approach extends beyond a biomanufacturing cell line to human immune cells.

Mechanistic interpretation

Demonstrated in the paper: Because each cell samples a different combination of parts, selection on an essential nutrient reports directly on which gene content, expression level and localization make a pathway work. The repeated enrichment of mitochondrially targeted enzymes identifies organellar localization as a design variable that rational approaches had largely left out.

Author hypotheses: The datasets generated this way are proposed as training data for machine-learning models that decode and design complex biosynthetic traits in mammalian systems, rather than as a finished design rule.

Limitations and uncertainties

  • The work is in vitro engineering validation; no in vivo or therapeutic readout is reported.
  • How well a pathway transfers between cell lines, how stable the integrated DNA remains over time, and what the approach costs at manufacturing scale are left to later work.
  • The method requires a phenotype that can be selected for, which constrains the traits that can be screened this way.

Clinical and industry implications

For cell therapy and biomanufacturing, a chassis that makes its own essential nutrients could simplify media, lower cost and make engineered cells less dependent on their environment. More broadly, the result shows that pathway-scale engineering in mammalian cells can be screened rather than hand-designed, which changes what is reasonable to attempt in a mammalian chassis.
