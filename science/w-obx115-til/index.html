Regulatable IL-15 engineered TIL therapy in ICI-resistant melanoma: RP2D ORR 66.7% (10/15)

Amsokigene autoleucel (OBX-115) expresses acetazolamide-regulatable membrane-bound IL-15, removing the need for exogenous IL-2. In pooled phase 1 and 1/2 data in immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)-resistant advanced melanoma, 10 of 15 patients at the recommended phase 2 dose (RP2D) achieved objective responses (66.7%; 95% CI 38–88%), including two complete responses. No dose-limiting toxicities, ICU admissions, or treatment-related deaths occurred.

Study: Two single-arm early-phase clinical trials

Key data card

  • Study type: Human; two single-arm early-phase clinical trials, pooled analysis
  • Patients: 37 treated patients with checkpoint-inhibitor-resistant advanced melanoma
  • Product: Amsokigene autoleucel (amsoki-cel/OBX-115)
  • Mechanism: Acetazolamide-regulatable membrane-bound IL-15
  • ORR at RP2D: 10/15 patients (66.7%; 95% CI 38–88%), including 2 complete responses
  • Grade ≥3 TEAEs: Neutropenia 24/37 (64.9%), leukopenia 21/37 (56.8%), lymphopenia 21/37 (56.8%) — primarily from lymphodepleting chemotherapy
  • Safety: No DLTs, no ICU admissions, no treatment-related deaths; serious TEAEs 10/37
  • Evidence level: Early clinical; small, nonrandomized, pooled dataset

Background and open questions

Tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) therapy has shown clinical activity in advanced melanoma, but current regimens require high-dose exogenous IL-2 to support T-cell expansion and persistence, which causes substantial toxicity including capillary leak syndrome. Whether a pharmacologically controllable cytokine signal built into the cell product could support TIL function without systemic IL-2 remained untested in humans.

Study design

The investigators conducted two single-arm early-phase trials (phase 1 and phase 1/2) in patients with checkpoint-inhibitor-resistant advanced melanoma. Amsokigene autoleucel (OBX-115) is an autologous TIL product engineered to express a membrane-bound form of IL-15 whose activity is switched on by the small-molecule drug acetazolamide. Patients received lymphodepleting chemotherapy followed by TIL infusion without exogenous IL-2. The pooled analysis evaluated 37 treated patients, with 15 at the recommended phase 2 dose (RP2D).

Key results

  • At the RP2D (Key Efficacy Set: phase 1 DL3 plus phase 2, 15 patients), 10 achieved objective responses (66.7%; 95% CI 38–88%), including 2 complete responses; in the phase 2 cohort alone (9 patients), ORR was 6/9 (66.7%).
  • The first patient experienced rapid lymphocyte expansion and edema around a previously irradiated brain metastasis (not meeting DLT criteria), prompting protocol modifications: maximum cell dose reduced 80%, acetazolamide dose reduced 75%.
  • Common grade ≥3 treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) included neutropenia (64.9%), leukopenia (56.8%), and lymphopenia (56.8%), primarily attributable to lymphodepleting chemotherapy.
  • No dose-limiting toxicities, ICU admissions, or treatment-related deaths occurred; serious TEAEs occurred in 10/37 patients.
  • Median follow-up was 8.3 months; median PFS was 8.1 months.

Mechanistic interpretation

The data provide human proof-of-concept that a membrane-bound cytokine signal under small-molecule control can support TIL expansion and antitumor activity without the systemic toxicity of high-dose IL-2. The acetazolamide-dependent switch offers a potential safety lever if uncontrolled expansion occurs.

Limitations

  • The dataset is small and nonrandomized; the pooled analysis (post hoc) does not establish comparative efficacy versus approved TIL products or other melanoma therapies.
  • These are early-phase data; durability, long-term safety, and efficacy in larger populations remain to be determined.
  • The dose-adjustment history after the first patient's adverse event (which did not meet DLT criteria) underscores that the optimal regimen is still being refined.
