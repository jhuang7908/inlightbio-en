MDT-BRIDGE: neoadjuvant durvalumab plus chemotherapy, then surgery or chemoradiotherapy by team decision

In the phase 2 MDT-BRIDGE trial, 142 patients with resectable or borderline resectable stage IIB–IIIB NSCLC received neoadjuvant durvalumab plus chemotherapy, then surgery or chemoradiotherapy as decided by a multidisciplinary team. The resection rate was 74.6%, pCR was 27.3% in the 121-patient resectable cohort, and 12-month EFS was 88.5%.

By Jing Huang, PhD · Generated: Nov 1, 2026

Key data card

  • Study type: Phase 2, single-arm, multicentre
  • Sample size n: 142
  • Population: Resectable or borderline resectable stage IIB–IIIB NSCLC
  • Intervention: Neoadjuvant durvalumab + platinum chemotherapy; then surgery or CRT by MDT decision
  • Resection rate: 74.6% (95% CI 66.7–81.6); 81.5% if resectable at baseline
  • pCR: 27.3% in resectable cohort (n=121)
  • 12-month EFS: 88.5% (95% CI 81.5–92.9); maturity 14.1%
  • Safety: Neoadjuvant grade 3/4 treatment-related AEs 25.9% (resectable) and 17.9% (unresectable cohort)
  • Evidence level: Single-arm phase 2; no randomized control
  • Mechanism: PD-L1 blockade with chemotherapy before local therapy; the MDT re-assesses resectability after treatment.

Background and open questions

Perioperative chemoimmunotherapy is standard for resectable NSCLC, but many stage III patients are borderline resectable and may instead receive chemoradiotherapy. The trial asked how patients fare when a multidisciplinary team chooses the local treatment after neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy.

Study design

Patients with stage IIB–IIIB disease received neoadjuvant durvalumab plus chemotherapy. A multidisciplinary team then assigned surgery or chemoradiotherapy, followed by adjuvant or consolidation durvalumab. ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT05925530.

Key results

  • Overall resection rate was 74.6% (95% CI 66.7–81.6); 81.5% among patients resectable at baseline.
  • pCR was 27.3% in the 121-patient resectable cohort (31.3% resectable at baseline vs 18.4% borderline).
  • 12-month EFS was 88.5% (95% CI 81.5–92.9) at 14.1% maturity (20 of 142 events).
  • Grade 3/4 treatment-related AEs in the neoadjuvant phase occurred in 25.9% and 17.9% of the resectable and unresectable cohorts.

Mechanistic interpretation

The results suggest neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy can be given before the final local-treatment decision, with team review then choosing surgery or chemoradiotherapy.

Limitations

  • Single-arm design with no randomized comparator.
  • EFS is immature (14.1% maturity); longer follow-up is needed.
  • MDT decisions may vary between centres.
