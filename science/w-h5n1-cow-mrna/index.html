H5 mRNA–LNP vaccine cuts H5N1 virus in milk about 1,000-fold in lactating dairy cows

In a challenge study with 8 lactating dairy cows (4 vaccinated, 4 controls), an intramuscular H5 mRNA–LNP vaccine with a boost 3 weeks later induced HA-reactive and neutralizing serum antibodies and HA-reactive antibodies in milk. After intramammary H5N1 challenge, peak viral RNA in milk was about 1,000-fold lower, with less mastitis and mammary fibrosis.

By Jing Huang, PhD · Generated: Nov 1, 2026

Citation

Santos JJS, Souza CK, Zanella GC, Goulart DB, Arruda B, Boggiatto P, et al. An mRNA–lipid nanoparticle vaccine against H5N1 influenza virus protects lactating dairy cows from infection. Nat Biotechnol. 2026 Oct 28. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-026-03343-6

Primary field: Vaccines & infection immunology · Related: H5N1 influenza, Dairy cattle, mRNA–LNP vaccine, Veterinary vaccines, One Health

About the authors

22 authors; see the paper for affiliations.

Corresponding authors: Gary C. Althouse, Amy L. Baker, Scott E. Hensley

Key data card

  • Study type: Animal vaccination and challenge study
  • Sample size n: 8 lactating cows (4 vaccinated, 4 controls)
  • Vaccine: mRNA–LNP encoding HA of A/dairy cattle/Texas/24-008749-002/2024
  • Schedule: Intramuscular prime, boost 3 weeks later
  • Challenge: Intramammary H5N1 inoculation
  • Primary result: ~1,000-fold lower peak viral RNA in milk
  • Other outcomes: Less mastitis and mammary fibrosis
  • Evidence level: Small animal study; not a field trial
  • Mechanism: vaccine-induced HA antibodies, including in milk, limit viral replication in the mammary gland.

Background and open questions

H5N1 has spread among US dairy cattle since 2024, with high viral loads in milk. It was unknown whether vaccination could protect the mammary gland.

Study design

Four lactating cows received an H5 mRNA–LNP vaccine intramuscularly with a boost three weeks later; four served as unvaccinated controls. Cows were then challenged by intramammary inoculation with H5N1.

Key results

  • Vaccination elicited HA-reactive and neutralizing antibodies in serum and HA-reactive antibodies in milk.
  • After challenge, milk viral RNA was significantly lower in vaccinated cows at multiple time points, with ~1,000-fold lower peak titers.
  • Vaccinated cows had less mastitis and mammary fibrosis.

Mechanistic interpretation

Antibodies at the mammary gland are a plausible route to reducing virus shed in milk and onward spread on farms.

Limitations

  • Very small groups (n=4 per arm).
  • Experimental intramammary challenge may not match natural farm transmission.
  • Duration of protection and field effectiveness were not tested.
