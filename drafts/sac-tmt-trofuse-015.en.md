## At a glance

On 20 November 2026, Merck announced that the pivotal Phase 3 TroFuse-015 trial (NCT06356311), in patients with locally advanced unresectable or metastatic gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma who had received two or more prior lines of systemic therapy, met its primary endpoint of overall survival (OS), with a statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvement versus treatment of physician’s choice (TPC). The study enrolled 473 patients, randomized to sacituzumab tirumotecan (sac-TMT), an investigational TROP2-directed antibody-drug conjugate, or TPC. The release does not disclose a hazard ratio, median OS, confidence interval, or P value. A secondary endpoint, progression-free survival (PFS) by blinded independent central review, was not met. Objective response rate nominally favored sac-TMT but was not formally tested under the pre-specified analysis plan.

## Key facts (Merck press release only)

- Drug: sacituzumab tirumotecan (sac-TMT), developed by Kelun-Biotech; Merck holds exclusive rights to develop, manufacture and commercialize the product outside Greater China (mainland China, Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan).
- Trial: randomized, open-label, multicenter, global pivotal Phase 3 TroFuse-015.
- Population: locally advanced unresectable or metastatic gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma (the release states this includes gastric, gastroesophageal junction and esophageal adenocarcinoma) after two or more prior systemic regimens.
- Enrollment: 473 patients randomized to sac-TMT or TPC (trifluridine/tipiracil, irinotecan, paclitaxel or docetaxel).
- Dosing: sac-TMT 4 mg/kg on days 1, 15 and 29 of each 6-week cycle.
- Primary endpoint, OS: the final analysis showed a statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvement versus TPC; the release says results were generally consistent across subgroups, regardless of TROP2 expression status.
- Not disclosed: HR, median OS, CI, or P value. Data are planned for presentation at an upcoming medical meeting and for submission to regulators.
- Secondary endpoints: BICR-assessed PFS was not met. ORR nominally favored sac-TMT but was not formally tested — it should not be written as a positive secondary endpoint.
- Safety: consistent with previously reported data; no new safety signals.
- The release states this is the first time a TROP2 ADC has shown an OS improvement versus TPC in this population in a global Phase 3 trial, and the second positive Phase 3 readout in the TroFuse global program, which at the time included 18 ongoing global Phase 3 trials.

## Writing notes

- Do not turn “clinically meaningful” into a specific number of months.
- Do not treat the untested ORR as a positive secondary endpoint.
- Until the numerical results are presented, keep the wording to “the primary endpoint was met; effect-size figures await the meeting disclosure.”
