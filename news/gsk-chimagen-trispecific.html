Summary

GSK acquired global rights from Chimagen to a trispecific T-cell engager for multiple myeloma: one arm binds T cells and the other two bind two undisclosed tumor-associated antigens. It is still preclinical; GSK expects it to enter Phase 1 in 2027. The dual-antigen design targets the antigen loss and relapse commonly seen after single-target T-cell engagers (such as BCMA) and CAR-T. This is the second time GSK has bought an asset from Chimagen; the earlier CMG1A46 is a different molecule. Together with Roche's September in-licensing of Simcere Zaiming's CD79a×CD19×CD3, trispecifics are becoming a new deal hotspot in antibody engineering.
