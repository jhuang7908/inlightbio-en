This week's deals: Sanofi pays $41 billion for Insmed, while upfront cash is just 4.6% of the headline in a Chinese ADC license

By Rocky Huang (黄菁) · November 28, 2026 (U.S. Eastern)

Source

Company announcements and public reporting, November 17–25, 2026. Sanofi press release ↗

Four deals worth recording this week: two where the buyer paid for certainty, one where a Chinese asset was bought again, and one where capital kept backing a platform.

Sanofi / Insmed: $41.0 billion in cash, $175 per share (November 24). Sanofi agreed to buy Insmed for $175 per share in cash, about $41.0 billion in equity value and a 46% premium to the November 18 close, the day before media reports of a possible deal. Closing is expected in the first half of 2027, subject to a two-thirds Insmed shareholder vote and regulatory clearances. The package is two marketed products, Brinsupri (brensocatib) and Arikayce, plus a Phase 3 inhaled treprostinil palmitil (TPIP); Sanofi expects more than $500 million in annual cost synergies and EPS accretion by the end of 2028. It is the largest pharma deal since Pfizer–Seagen ($43 billion, December 2023). What it says: Sanofi is buying time ahead of Dupixent's loss of exclusivity (analysts estimate 2031–32). Jefferies put it bluntly: the deal "does not fix R&D/pipeline questions, but it buys time." Buyers will pay up for assets that already sell.

Hansoh / Bayer: $150 million upfront, up to $3.1 billion in milestones (November 25). Hansoh granted Bayer exclusive rights to HS-20122, an EGFR/c-Met bispecific ADC, outside mainland China, Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan. Hansoh receives $150 million upfront, up to $3.1 billion in development, regulatory and commercial milestones, and tiered royalties; the agreement takes effect after antitrust clearance (U.S. HSR). The molecule has completed only a Phase 1, with data presented at ESMO 2026. Upfront is about 4.6% of upfront plus milestones ($3.25 billion). What it says: buyers are coming back to the same Chinese company. Fierce reports that Roche signed an ADC portfolio pact with Hansoh last month (billed at about $15 billion) and GSK is already advancing two Hansoh ADCs. The cash-royalty-milestone structure is also the cleanest one under Treasury's proposed COINS rule (see this week's lead).

Lilly / LoQus23: up to $725 million in cash, upfront undisclosed (November 23). Lilly acquired Cambridge, UK-based LoQus23 Therapeutics for up to $725 million, covering an upfront payment and later development and regulatory milestones; neither side disclosed the upfront. The lead asset, LQT-23, is an oral allosteric MSH3 inhibitor meant to slow somatic expansion of CAG repeats in Huntington's disease. It is preclinical, and the company expects to file a clinical trial application by the end of 2026. What it says: $725 million is a ceiling, not what Lilly paid today. A preclinical oral small molecule with a disease-modifying thesis can still be bought outright, which shows the somatic-expansion approach is being taken seriously.

Outside this week (addendum, November 17) · Isomorphic Labs: $5 billion Series C. The DeepMind spinout raised $5 billion, co-led by Greenoaks and Sequoia with a16z as a new investor and participation from Alphabet and all existing investors, less than six months after its $2.1 billion Series B in May. The company did not disclose a valuation; the roughly $50 billion valuation is unconfirmed by the company and comes from Bloomberg's report of talks. It says internal oncology and immunology programs have produced preclinical results "setting the stage for clinical entry"; it has not announced a clinical trial. What it says: private capital will still back a platform that has no drug in humans at this price. That is confidence, not proof.

My read of the week

I am wary of the phrase "BD is recovering." I have no year-over-year data and would not claim it. Three things I can say.

First, headline totals run well ahead of real cash. About 95% of Hansoh's roughly $3.25 billion headline is contingent on milestones, and Lilly did not disclose its upfront. Read the upfront, not the total.

Second, large pharma is a repeat customer for Chinese assets, not an experimenter. Roche, GSK and Bayer have all gone to Hansoh, and what they buy are ADC and bispecific formats where a Chinese developer has early data in hand. I think the ADC and bispecific window is still open, but its shape is changing: Treasury's proposed COINS rule makes anything beyond cash upfront plus milestones harder, so structure itself is becoming the next negotiating point.

Third, the biggest check this week, $41 billion, bought certainty, not new science. Early assets still find buyers (Lilly) and platforms still find investors (Isomorphic), but the largest check went to a company that already sells drugs. For those building early assets, that is not bad news: the cash is plentiful, but buyers want data to remove the uncertainty before they pay a high price.
