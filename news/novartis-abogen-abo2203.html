Novartis takes global rights to Abogen's in vivo mRNA T-cell engager ABO2203

Illustration (decorative, not data).
Headline terms

$575M upfront; up to ~$7.8B total

$575M upfront + up to ~$7.2B in milestones (including all option exercises) + sales royalties

Source

Edition note

Translated from the Chinese edition 前沿追踪 · 经济动态 (original title: 诺华拿下艾博生物 mRNA 体内 T 细胞衔接器 ABO2203 全球权益). No facts added in translation.

Summary

ABO2203 is not an in vivo CAR-T. It uses lipid nanoparticles to deliver mRNA encoding a CD19×CD3 T-cell engager, so that the patient's own cells “manufacture” the bispecific to clear B cells. It is in a Phase 1 study in refractory autoimmune disease; the first data made public cover 3 patients with refractory secondary immune thrombocytopenia. Novartis also receives an exclusive option on next-generation assets from Abogen's mRNA/LNP platform. Notably, a $575 million upfront is high for an early Phase 1 asset, suggesting big pharma is willing to bet early on “biologics generated in vivo”, and that mRNA companies have found a new outlet beyond vaccines in autoimmunity.

Deal terms, as disclosed

$575M upfront + up to ~$7.2B in milestones (including all option exercises) + sales royalties.
