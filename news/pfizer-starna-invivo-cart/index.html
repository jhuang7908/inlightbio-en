Pfizer in-licenses three preclinical in vivo CAR-T programs from Starna, with $330 million upfront

By InLight Editorial · November 18, 2026 (U.S. Eastern); company name checked against Starna’s site on November 23, 2026

On November 18, 2026, Pfizer announced that it had entered into an exclusive licensing agreement with Starna Therapeutics, a biotechnology company developing RNA medicines designed to enable in vivo therapeutics. Under the agreement, Pfizer obtains exclusive rights outside Greater China to develop, manufacture and commercialize three pre-IND in vivo chimeric antigen receptor T-cell (CAR-T) programs focused on treating autoimmune diseases. Starna will receive an upfront payment of $330 million and is eligible for up to $2.3 billion in milestone payments associated with certain development and commercial milestones, plus royalties on net sales of approved products.

Timeline

  • Starna Therapeutics Co., Ltd. (星锐医药（苏州）有限公司) presents itself on its official site as 星锐医药, describing an extrahepatic targeted lipid nanoparticle (LNP) platform and an RNA pipeline that includes three preclinical in vivo cell-therapy programs for autoimmune disease (STR-P004, STR-P005 and STR-P006).
  • November 18, 2026 (Wednesday, 6:45 a.m. ET): Pfizer issued the press release announcing the exclusive license.
  • This site posted a deals-feed card on November 18, 2026. On November 23, the Chinese name on that card was aligned with Starna’s official site (星锐医药), and this standalone brief was added. No second card was created.

Key facts

According to Pfizer’s release, the company obtains exclusive rights outside Greater China to three pre-IND in vivo CAR-T programs for autoimmune disease. Pfizer also receives rights to use Starna’s proprietary in vivo CAR-T technology in inflammation and immunology, and in oncology, as well as options to exclusively license compounds created under a separate research collaboration, and an option to expand the territory to include exclusive rights to develop and commercialize each exclusively licensed compound and product in Greater China.

On financial terms, Starna will receive $330 million upfront and is eligible for up to $2.3 billion in milestone payments associated with certain development and commercial milestones, plus royalties on net sales of approved products. The release does not disclose royalty tiers, the targets or specific indications of the three programs, or an expected timeline for first-in-human studies.

Pfizer will lead clinical development outside Greater China and plans to manufacture the finished drug product for the initial clinical trials using its facilities in the United States. Pfizer will have sole and exclusive control over commercialization outside Greater China for all products. The Greater China expansion remains an option; the release does not say that option has been exercised.

Michael Vincent, M.D., Ph.D., Pfizer’s chief inflammation and immunology officer, said in the release that in vivo CAR-T is an emerging approach for treating autoimmune diseases, that the patients who might benefit most are those for whom today’s options fall short, and that Pfizer has experience developing and manufacturing RNA-based medicines. Rongkuan Hu, Ph.D., chief executive officer of Starna, said the company’s targeted LNP delivery platform and RNA technology provide a foundation for advancing in vivo CAR-T, and that Starna is joining forces with Pfizer to bring therapies to patients worldwide.

The official pipeline and the licensed assets should be kept distinct: Starna’s site currently lists three preclinical in vivo cell-therapy programs for autoimmune disease (STR-P004, STR-P005 and STR-P006). Pfizer’s release refers to “three pre-IND in vivo CAR-T programs” and does not name those codes, so the website codes should not be treated as confirmed licensed assets.

This brief is based primarily on Pfizer’s November 18 release; Starna’s official site was checked on November 23, 2026 for the Chinese company name and pipeline listing. The release does not include clinical-trial data.

Significance

This is a large license of preclinical in vivo CAR-T assets, connecting Starna’s platform and three autoimmune programs to Pfizer’s inflammation and immunology pipeline, with Pfizer leading clinical development and initial trial-material manufacturing outside Greater China. The disclosed amounts — $330 million upfront and up to $2.3 billion in milestones — are as stated in the announcement, but the programs remain pre-IND, so whether they enter the clinic and whether the milestones are earned is still ahead. Greater China rights are an option, not a completed grant of China commercial rights.

Commentary · Rocky

The appeal of in vivo CAR-T is that it does not require a bespoke cell product for each patient. What this agreement actually buys is three still-preclinical (pre-IND) programs, plus rights to use the platform in inflammation, immunology and oncology — not human data already read out. A $330 million upfront is large for an autoimmunity license, but whether the $2.3 billion milestone cap is ever earned depends on whether these programs reach and complete the clinic. The release also includes an option on Greater China; that should be read separately from what has already been granted: exclusive development and commercialization outside Greater China. Aligning the Chinese name with Starna’s official site (星锐医药) is a source check, not a change in the deal itself.
